“The AI can’t draw” — first-principles teardown

Why the hand kept failing, and the pipelines that fix it

Verdict: the loop treated a choice as a law. Nothing in physics requires the model to author the shape — only that a shape exists and reaches the HTML. Every dead end came from assigning the AI the one role it is structurally worst at (source of organic geometry) instead of the role it is best at (transform of a source into the design). Swap the roles and the problem dissolves.

Why it failed — physics, not skill

  • No spatial buffer. An LLM emits tokens. Producing a hand means emitting a long Bezier coordinate sequence that happens to trace a hand — an inverse map from a visual concept to numbers, executed blind. A master illustrator given only a path-coordinate keyboard fails the same way. This is architecture, not intelligence.
  • Granularity mismatch. The screenshot loop helps, but errors live in space while fixes live in tokens. Each round repairs one glitch and births two more — slow convergence by construction. Observed: multiple rounds on the silhouette alone, hatching that “did nothing”, then a revert.
  • Blindness is not how anyone draws. No professional produces organic anatomy without a reference under the pen. The workflow asked the model to do something strictly harder than what illustrators do.

What designers actually do (researched)

  • Trace over reference. Standard pipeline: photo or sketch on a locked layer at 50% opacity → draw over it. Studios snap a webcam pic of their own hand as reference. Nobody draws blind. verified
  • Primitives + booleans. Flat vector work is circles/rects combined by union/subtract/intersect — placement and arithmetic, not freehand control. verified
  • Image trace + cleanup. Illustrator’s Image Trace, Inkscape’s Trace Bitmap (potrace engine) — raster in, paths out, then tidy anchors. verified
  • Source → transform split. The drawing and the vectorizing are always two steps with a tool between them. The loop skipped the tool and asked tokens to do the tool’s job. verified

Pipelines that work in this repo

A — Sketch → trace → integrate (recommended for the hand)

You draw the hand once (paper photo is fine — uneven lighting is a solved problem: adaptive threshold). The agent runs:

threshold (PIL) → potrace/vtracer → svgo → path into the u-unit SVG

Deterministic math, not drawing skill. Potrace is the same engine Inkscape ships; alphamax controls curve smoothing. One install needed — nothing on this machine traces today verified gap.

B — Photo → vector, no drawing at all

Pick any hand photo you like the pose of → vtracer --preset bw or edge-extract → outline paths. The reference you already pasted could have been traced instead of “looked at” — looking then writing coordinates is still blind drawing; threshold→contour is a transform.

C — Keep it raster

Physics never required vectors. A transparent PNG + CSS mask, mix-blend-mode, or opacity treatment can be the final asset. Simplest possible pipeline; the “must be SVG” requirement was a choice.

D — Hybrid: your silhouette + AI’s parametric detail

You supply only the outer contour. Veins and labels are curves inside a known boundary — a branching-stroke pattern clipped by clipPath to the hand path. That is math on a known container, not blind drawing. Hatching as a <pattern> + mask is parametric too — tunable blind, which is likely why hand-placed hatching failed where this would not.

The division-of-labor rule: a human hand, a photo, a file, or an image model supplies the source. The AI owns every step that is a transform — trace, simplify, recolor, mask, clip, blend, animate, layout, responsive u scaling, procedural texture (feTurbulence, stipple fields, hatch patterns). Source once; transform forever.

Open gaps

  • No tracer installed — one apt install potrace or a vtracer binary away. needs your approval
  • Trace quality on this specific hand image is untested — 5 min to verify once a tool exists. assumption
  • Image-gen source needs wiring; optional, and lowest fidelity to your vision anyway.
  • Veins, labels, and your unstated “other idea” from the last session are still open — all of them become tractable once the silhouette is a real path.

Pick a pipeline and it runs. For the hand: A or B.
